The question

PROMPT: Using all three documents and your knowledge of the Great Depression (1929–1941), construct an argument that answers the following question: How did competing diagnoses of the Great Depression's causes reflect fundamentally different visions of the government's proper role in the economy? In your response, you MUST: 1. Analyze each document using the HIPP framework (Historical context, Intended audience, Purpose, Point of view)

  1. at least one HIPP element per document. 2. Identify the different diagnoses of the Depression offered by each document and connect them to the speaker's broader ideological position. 3. Construct a central argument that uses evidence from all three documents AND at least two pieces of outside historical knowledge to explain how these competing diagnoses shaped the policy debate of the 1930s. 4. Evaluate which diagnosis
  2. Hoover's, FDR's, or Eccles's
  3. most accurately accounted for the structural causes of the Depression and explain why

Scoring guide

Pts6-point scale
6Exemplary. All three documents analyzed with at least one precise HIPP element each (historical context accurately identified for all three). Central argument is clear, defensible, and directly answers the question about competing diagnoses and government role. Uses evidence from all three documents AND at least two pieces of accurate outside historical knowledge. Evaluates which diagnosis is most accurate with specific reasoning. Writing is coherent, well-organized, and historically rigorous.
5Advanced. Three documents analyzed with HIPP elements (may miss one element). Central argument is clear and uses all three documents plus at least two outside facts. Evaluation of which diagnosis is most accurate is present but reasoning is partially developed.
4Proficient. Two documents analyzed with HIPP (third may be missing or superficial). Argument uses evidence from at least two documents and one outside fact. Connects diagnoses to government role with adequate reasoning. Evaluation present but may lack depth.
3Adequate. At least two documents referenced. Argument addresses the question but evidence use is uneven — may rely heavily on one document. HIPP analysis is present for at least one document. Some outside knowledge present.
2Developing. Only one or two documents referenced with minimal analysis. Central argument is unclear or only partially addresses the prompt. Limited use of outside knowledge. Little or no HIPP analysis.
1Beginning. Response summarizes one document without analysis. No argument constructed. No HIPP analysis. No outside knowledge.
0No response or completely off-topic.
Expected evidence and document analysis. DOCUMENT ANALYSIS EXPECTATIONS: - Document A (Hoover): Context = post-Black Thursday; Audience = public/markets; Purpose = reassurance; POV = market self-correction, limited government - Document B (FDR): Context = Depression nadir 1933; Audience = national public; Purpose = reframe Depression as moral failure of speculation; POV = moral-structural critique of laissez-faire capitalism - Document C (Eccles): Context = 1935 Banking Act debate; Audience = Senate; Purpose = advocate demand-side intervention; POV = proto-Keynesian / underconsumption theory KEY EVIDENCE EXPECTED: Stock market speculation and margin buying; Black Tuesday (October 29, 1929); bank failures (9,000+, 1930–1933); Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930); Federal Reserve monetary contraction; 25% unemployment (1933); Hoover's voluntarism and RFC; First New Deal programs (NIRA, AAA, CCC); Second New Deal (Social Security, Wagner Act) as demand-side response.
