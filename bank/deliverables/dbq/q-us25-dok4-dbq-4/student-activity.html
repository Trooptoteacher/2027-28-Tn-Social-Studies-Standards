The Road to War — American Neutrality and Entry into World War I, 1914–1917

US.26 — Explain the reasons for U.S. entry into World War I, including the use of unrestricted submarine warfare, the Zimmerman Telegram, the defense of democracy, and economic motivations.

US.27 — Identify and explain the impact of the following on World War I (T.C.A. § 49-6-1006): • Trench warfare • Use of new weapons and technology • John J. Pershing • Harlem Hell Fighters • Alvin C. York

Name _______________________________ Class ____________ Date ____________

Directions. Read the three documents below. Then write a well-organized essay addressing the following question: To what extent was U.S. entry into World War I in April 1917 a response to deliberate foreign provocation versus a product of the United States' own prior choices and entanglements? Your essay must: — Apply SOAPS analysis to at least two documents — Use evidence from all three documents — Incorporate outside knowledge of U.S. neutrality policy 1914–1917 — Construct a clear, defensible argument

The sources

Document 1

The Zimmermann Telegram, January 16, 1917 — U.S. National Archives, public domain (decoded and released by British intelligence to the U.S. State Department, February 24, 1917)

We intend to begin on the first of February unrestricted submarine warfare. We shall endeavor in spite of this to keep the United States of America neutral. In the event of this not succeeding, we make Mexico the following offer to alliance on the following basis: make war together, make peace together, generous financial support and an understanding on our part that Mexico is to reconquer the lost territory in Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona. The settlement in detail is left to you. You will inform the President of the above most secretly as soon as the outbreak of war with the United States is certain..." — Arthur Zimmermann, German Foreign Secretary

Document 2

President Woodrow Wilson, Address to Congress Requesting Declaration of War, April 2, 1917 — Bryan, William Jennings, ed., Republic or Empire (1899), public domain, public domain

The present German submarine warfare against commerce is a warfare against mankind. It is a war against all nations. American ships have been sunk, American lives taken, in ways which it has stirred us very deeply to learn of, but the ships and people of other neutral and friendly nations have been sunk and overwhelmed in the waters in the same way. There has been no discrimination. The challenge is to all mankind.

Document 3

Senator George Norris of Nebraska, Speech Opposing U.S. Declaration of War, April 4, 1917 — Bryan, William Jennings, ed., Republic or Empire (1899), public domain, public domain

We are going into war upon the command of gold... I feel that we are about to put the dollar sign on the American flag... I am confident that the object of the President, in asking for war, is to serve the wealthy interests of our country and not the plain people... I think we should wait patiently until the American people are willing to go into war for their country rather than for the bankers.
