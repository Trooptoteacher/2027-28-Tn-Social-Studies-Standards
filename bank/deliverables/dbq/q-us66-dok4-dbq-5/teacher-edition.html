Document A

President John F. Kennedy, Television and Radio Address on Cuba, October 22, 1962 — U.S. government document, available through the John F. Kennedy Presidential Library (National Archives)

The Soviet Union has taken a new and reckless step in raising the threat of thermonuclear war. Acting in the defense of our own security and of the entire Western Hemisphere, and under the authority entrusted to me by the Constitution as endorsed by the resolution of the Congress, I have directed that the following initial steps be taken immediately. First: To halt this offensive buildup, a strict quarantine on all offensive military equipment under shipment to Cuba is being initiated.

The question

Using all three documents and your knowledge of the Cuban Missile Crisis (US.66), construct an argument analyzing the diplomatic resolution of the Crisis as a case study in nuclear deterrence and crisis management. Apply HIPP analysis to at least two documents and

  1. what Kennedy's and Khrushchev's rhetoric reveals about the constraints and rationality of nuclear deterrence
  2. what the episode reveals about the difference between public and private diplomacy in nuclear crises

Expected evidence and document analysis. DOCUMENT ANALYSIS EXPECTATIONS: Document A HIPP — 'quarantine' vs. 'blockade' terminology as legal/political calculation; the simultaneous public/Soviet audience. Document B HIPP — Khrushchev's 'moles' metaphor as emotional authenticity contrasting with his public bluster; the Oct. 26 letter as the opening of the resolution path. Document C HIPP — Kennedy's conditional language as coercive bargaining; 'permanent solution to the Cuban problem' as face-saving vagueness. KEY EVIDENCE EXPECTED: ExComm (13 members, 13 days); quarantine announced Oct. 22; Khrushchev Oct. 26 and Oct. 27 letters (two different letters on Oct. 27 complicating resolution); U-2 shootdown Oct. 27; B-59 submarine incident; secret Turkey missile deal; hotline (June 1963); Limited Test Ban Treaty (Aug. 1963); SALT I (1972).
