The sources

Document 1

W.E.B. Du Bois, The Souls of Black Folk, Chapter I, 1903. Library of Congress and Project Gutenberg.

After the Egyptian and Indian, the Greek and Roman, the Teuton and Mongolian, the Negro is a sort of seventh son, born with a veil, and gifted with second-sight in this American world — a world which yields him no true self-consciousness, but only lets him see himself through the revelation of the other world. It is a peculiar sensation, this double-consciousness, this sense of always looking at one's self through the eyes of others, of measuring one's soul by the tape of a world that looks on in amused contempt and pity. One ever feels his two-ness — an American, a Negro; two souls, two thoughts, two unreconciled strivings; two warring ideals in one dark body, whose dogged strength alone keeps it from being torn asunder.

Scoring guide

Pts6-point scale
6Exemplary. Constructs a sophisticated argument about selective democratization that traces how democratic expansion for one group coexisted with racial exclusion. Applies SOAPS to all three documents with specific analysis of significance that connects each document to the selective democratization argument. Incorporates two or more outside historical facts. Demonstrates cross-document synthesis — e.g., connecting Du Bois's "double-consciousness" to the 19th Amendment's race-neutral language, or connecting Addams's disenfranchisement analogy to its blind spots about race. Evaluates both what the Progressive Era achieved and what it failed to achieve for different groups.
5Advanced. Strong argument with SOAPS of all three documents. Two outside facts. Cross-document synthesis present. Addresses both achievement and limitation but the synthesis is less fully developed.
4Proficient. Clear argument with SOAPS of at least two documents. One outside fact. Addresses both groups but the selective democratization argument is incomplete.
3Adequate. Takes a position but focuses primarily on one group (women or African Americans). SOAPS of one document. One outside fact.
2Developing. Summarizes the documents without a sustained argument about selective democratization. Limited SOAPS.
1Beginning. Lists document information without analysis.
0No response or off-topic.
Expected evidence and document analysis. DOCUMENT ANALYSIS EXPECTATIONS (SOAPS): Doc 1 — Du Bois: the "double-consciousness" concept as direct evidence of the psychological cost of political exclusion; the occasion (post-Plessy) as context for why formal Progressive reforms were insufficient for Black Americans. Doc 2 — 19th Amendment: the significance of what the amendment's language does NOT say — it addresses sex discrimination but not racial discrimination, leaving Black women doubly excluded. Doc 3 — Addams: the significance of Addams's parallel between gender and class exclusion without addressing racial exclusion — revealing the racial limits of white Progressive reform thought. KEY EVIDENCE EXPECTED: NAWSA's exclusion of Black women from the 1913 suffrage parade and Alice Paul's response to Ida B. Wells; continued disenfranchisement of Black women (and men) in Southern states 1920-1965; founding of NAACP (1909) as a response to Progressive Era racial exclusion; Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) as constitutional backdrop; Wilson's segregation of the federal civil service (1913) despite his Progressive credentials; the 17th Amendment (direct Senate election) and 16th Amendment (income tax) as structural reforms that largely bypassed racial inequality; Ida B. Wells's anti-lynching campaign and its exclusion from mainstream Progressive organizations.
Sourcing. Every document above carries its own citation as written in the item record. Verify each against the holding institution before classroom use — an unsourced primary source is worse than a missing one.
