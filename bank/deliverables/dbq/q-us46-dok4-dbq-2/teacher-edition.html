History Hack · Document-Based Question

Using the following two primary sources, construct a document-based argument that analyzes how Pearl Harbor transformed American public opinion and government policy from isolationism to total war mobilization.

US.48 — Explain the progression of key events and President Franklin D. Roosevelt’s response to world crises that lead to U.S. entry into World War II, including the Quarantine Speech, the Four Freedoms Speech, the Atlantic Charter, the Lend-Lease Act, and Pearl Harbor.

US.49 — Analyze the response of the United States to the plight of European Jews before the start of the war, the U.S. liberation of concentration camps during the war, and the immigration of Holocaust survivors after the war.

Document A

Yesterday, December 7th, 1941 — a date which will live in infamy — the United States of America was suddenly and deliberately attacked by naval and air forces of the Empire of Japan... No matter how long it may take us to overcome this premeditated invasion, the American people in their righteous might will win through to absolute victory.

The question

Using SOAPS (Speaker, Occasion, Audience, Purpose, Significance) analysis for each document and your knowledge of U.S. foreign policy, analyze how Roosevelt's rhetoric evolved from January 1941 to December 1941, and evaluate what that evolution reveals about the political and strategic factors shaping U.S. entry into World War II. Include at least two pieces of outside evidence. Address the relationship between FDR's pre-Pearl Harbor rhetoric (Document B) and the formal isolationist legal framework he was navigating.

2Developing. Minimal thesis. Documents described but SOAPS not applied. Little outside evidence.

Expected evidence and document analysis. DOCUMENT ANALYSIS EXPECTATIONS: Doc A SOAPS — Speaker as wartime leader, occasion as day after attack, purpose as securing war declaration, significance as nation-transforming moment. Doc B SOAPS — Speaker as politically constrained president, occasion as pre-Pearl Harbor State of Union, purpose as building ideological groundwork, significance as revealing FDR's pre-war strategic thinking. KEY EVIDENCE EXPECTED: Neutrality Acts (1935–1937); Lend-Lease Act (March 1941); destroyers-for-bases agreement; America First Committee; 388-1 Congressional vote; Germany/Italy declaration of war (December 11, 1941).
