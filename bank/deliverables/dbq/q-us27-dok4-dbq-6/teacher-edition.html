History Hack · Document-Based Question

Peacemaking and Its Consequences — The Treaty of Versailles and the Fourteen Points, 1918–1919

US.26 — Explain the reasons for U.S. entry into World War I, including the use of unrestricted submarine warfare, the Zimmerman Telegram, the defense of democracy, and economic motivations.

US.29 — Analyze the significance of President Woodrow Wilson’s contributions to the Treaty of Versailles, including the Fourteen Points, the causes and effects of the U.S. rejection of the League of Nations, and the subsequent impact on world politics.

Directions. Read the three documents below. Then write a well-organized essay addressing the following question: To what extent did the Treaty of Versailles fulfill, compromise, or betray Wilson's Fourteen Points, and what were the consequences of those compromises for world peace? Your essay must: — Apply HIPP or SOAPS analysis to at least two documents — Use all three documents as evidence — Incorporate outside knowledge of the Paris Peace Conference and subsequent events — Construct a clear, defensible argument

2Developing. Weak thesis. Uses documents without framework analysis.

Expected evidence and document analysis. DOCUMENT ANALYSIS EXPECTATIONS: — Doc 1 (Wilson's 14 Points): Note that Point V addresses colonial self-determination — the Paris Conference largely ignored this for non-European peoples; Point XIV established the League; historical context = Wilson hoped Germany would accept an armistice based on the 14 Points, which it did in November 1918 — only to find the treaty imposed punitive terms. — Doc 2 (War Guilt Clause): Note this contradicts Wilson's 'peace without victory' vision; reparations were set at $33 billion (1921 London Schedule); the clause provided Hitler with his central propaganda tool — the 'war guilt lie'; Point of View = Clemenceau's revanchism. — Doc 3 (Lodge): Note Lodge was NOT an isolationist per se — he supported an amended league; Article X objection was about congressional war powers; Wilson's refusal to accept any reservations led to the defeat; Lodge-Wilson personal animosity as a complicating factor. KEY EVIDENCE EXPECTED: Paris Peace Conference participants (Wilson, Clemenceau, Lloyd George, Orlando); 'Big Four'; Wilson's stroke (October 1919); Senate rejection (November 1919, March 1920); German reparations ($33 billion); territorial losses; League without U.S.; Weimar instability; Hitler's exploitation of the War Guilt Clause; League failures in 1930s (Manchuria 1931, Ethiopia 1935–36).
Sourcing. Every document above carries its own citation as written in the item record. Verify each against the holding institution before classroom use — an unsourced primary source is worse than a missing one.
