The sources

Document A

Tennessee Valley Authority Act, Section 1, May 18, 1933 (National Archives, public domain)

Be it enacted...for the especial purpose of bringing about in said Tennessee Valley and adjoining territory...the maximum amount of flood control; the maximum development of said Tennessee River for navigation purposes; the maximum generation of electric power consistent with flood control and navigation; the proper use of marginal lands; the proper method of reforestation of all lands in said drainage basin suitable for reforestation; and the economic and social well-being of the people living in said river basin.

Document B

FDR Message to Congress Recommending Establishment of the TVA, April 10, 1933 (FDR Presidential Library, public domain)

It is clear that the Muscle Shoals development is but a small part of the potential public usefulness of the entire Tennessee River...if we are careful and far-sighted, we can make it serve as a model for the whole nation... I suggest that this power development of war days be used not merely as an opportunity for the comfort and happiness of adjoining States but as a means of planning for the improvement of the broadest of physical, social, and economic conditions in the whole of the territory.

Document C

Statement of Wendell Willkie, President of Commonwealth and Southern Corporation (private utility), opposing TVA expansion, Congressional testimony, 1933 (Congressional Record, public domain)

The Tennessee Valley Authority is entering into direct competition with established private industry which has made enormous investments in good faith under the laws of the land. The power business is not a natural monopoly...The government should not compete with private enterprise in the distribution of electric power. This is not a question of power rates; it is a question of whether the government of the United States is going to socialize the electric utility industry.

The question

PROMPT: Using all three documents and your knowledge of the Tennessee Valley Authority (1933–1941) and the broader New Deal debate, construct an argument that answers: To what extent did the TVA represent a new model for the relationship between the federal government and regional economic development

  1. and why was it so ideologically contested? In your response, you MUST: 1. Analyze each document using the HIPP framework
  2. identify at least ONE HIPP element for each document (historical context, intended audience, purpose, or point of view). 2. Identify the purpose and scope of the TVA as presented in Documents A and B, and explain what made this vision of federal intervention genuinely new. 3. Evaluate Willkie's critique (Document C) using historical evidence
  3. was his characterization of the TVA as 'socialization' accurate, and what does this debate reveal about the limits of the New Deal consensus? 4. Use at least two pieces of outside historical knowledge to strengthen your argument about the TVA's historical significance in Tennessee and nationally
