Huey Long, "Share Our Wealth" Radio Address, February 23, 1934 (Congressional Record, public domain)

How many men ever went to a barbecue and would let one man take off the table what was intended for nine-tenths of the people to eat? The same is true of the old U.S.A. Any man with a thimble full of sense ought to know that if you make 3 persons work to give 1 person what the 3 have earned, that the 1 person is getting 75 percent of all that is produced and that the other 3 are only getting 25 percent of what they produce...We must limit the size of the big man's fortune.

The question

PROMPT: Using all three documents and your knowledge of New Deal opposition (1933–1941), construct an argument that answers: How did critics of the New Deal from BOTH the left and the right, as well as the judiciary, reflect deeper disagreements about the proper limits of federal power

  1. and what did these critiques ultimately reveal about the New Deal's lasting tensions? In your response, you MUST: 1. Analyze each document using the HIPP framework
  2. at least ONE HIPP element per document. 2. Identify whether each document represents left-wing, right-wing, or judicial criticism, and explain the core argument each critic made against the New Deal. 3. Construct a central argument using all three documents AND at least two pieces of outside historical knowledge that explains how the simultaneous left and right opposition to the New Deal reveals the ideological boundaries within which FDR was operating. 4. Evaluate: Did the critics ultimately weaken, redirect, or strengthen the New Deal
  3. and which critique proved most consequential for FDR's second term?

Scoring guide

Pts6-point scale
6Exemplary. All three documents analyzed with accurate HIPP elements. Correctly identifies Long as left-wing, Liberty League as right-wing, and Schechter as judicial criticism. Constructs a coherent central argument about the ideological middle ground FDR occupied. Uses all three documents plus at least two outside facts. Evaluates which critique was most consequential with specific, accurate reasoning (Court-packing plan, Second New Deal pivot). Writing is rigorous and well-organized.
5Advanced. Three documents analyzed with HIPP (one element may be imprecise). Central argument addresses the ideological spectrum with all three documents. At least one outside fact. Evaluation of consequences present with some depth.
4Proficient. Two or three documents referenced with some HIPP analysis. Argument addresses left/right/judicial spectrum. At least one outside fact. Evaluation present but underdeveloped.
3Adequate. At least two documents referenced. Argument identifies some dimensions of New Deal opposition. Limited HIPP analysis. Some outside knowledge.
2Developing. One or two documents referenced with minimal analysis. Argument is vague. Little outside knowledge.
1Beginning. One document summarized without analysis. No argument. No HIPP.
0No response or completely off-topic.
