  4. D. Democrats in Congress opposed court-packing because FDR had not consulted them before announcing the plan, and the lack of consultation was seen as an executive overreach that threatened the independence of the legislative branch.

4. The Truman Doctrine (1947) pledged American support for free peoples resisting subjugation, while the Marshall Plan (1947) committed roughly $13 billion to European economic reconstruction. How do these two initiatives together reflect a coherent and unified strategic logic for containing Soviet influence in postwar Europe?

  1. A. The Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan were complementary primarily through their combined military and security dimensions — both relied on American military guarantees to deter Soviet armed aggression, with economic aid functioning as a secondary incentive to maintain Western-aligned governments.
  2. B. The Truman Doctrine established a global ideological framework for containment, but the Marshall Plan was primarily motivated by American economic self-interest in creating prosperous European export markets — suggesting that strategic and commercial interests happened to align in this case rather than reflecting a single coherent anti-Soviet vision.
  3. C. The two initiatives reflected the strategic insight that communism thrived in conditions of economic desperation and political instability — military containment alone would be insufficient if poverty created conditions in which Soviet-backed political movements could gain genuine popular support, making economic reconstruction a strategic necessity rather than purely humanitarian charity.
  4. D. Truman and Marshall designed their respective programs as deliberate provocations intended to force the Soviet Union into an unsustainable military competition, representing early applications of the rollback strategy later associated with Eisenhower's New Look defense policy.

The Act's passage required the convergence of sustained grassroots mobilization that disrupted segregation's daily functioning, television coverage that made white violence against nonviolent protesters politically costly to tolerate, and presidential leadership committed to legislative action — demonstrating that transformative civil rights legislation emerges from the alignment of movement pressure, public opinion shifts, and executive political
  2. B. The Act's passage shows that economic modernization in the South had eroded the material foundation of segregation by creating a business class that recognized international competitiveness required abandoning racial discrimination, making economic development the primary structural driver of civil rights legislation.
  3. C. The Act demonstrated that moral argument alone can overcome entrenched racial hierarchies, as the movement's nonviolent discipline and the moral clarity of its witness ultimately convinced a sufficient congressional majority to act despite fierce Southern opposition.
  4. D. The Civil Rights Act passed primarily because of Lyndon Johnson's legislative mastery and his exploitation of the national emotional response to Kennedy's assassination, suggesting that individual political leadership and historically contingent events were more decisive than structural conditions in producing the legislation.
