classroom-formative · pre-field-test. Item parameters are estimates and have not met a student; this form is not a calibrated instrument. This is a selected-response form. It assesses Depth of Knowledge levels 1 through 3. It carries no extended-response item, so it does not assess DOK-4 — a four-option question cannot ask a student to construct and defend an argument.   1. A. Because the New Deal greatly expanded federal regulation of the economy and created public employment and Social Security

  3. C. Because FDR eliminated private property rights by redistributing land from wealthy Americans to the poor
  4. D. Because FDR called for nationalizing all major American industries and placing them under government ownership

2. Critics of the New Deal from the left, including Huey Long and socialist intellectuals, argued it preserved capitalism without genuine structural transformation, while critics from the right argued it represented dangerous federal overreach. What does the existence of this two-sided criticism most reveal about the New Deal's actual ideological character and historical significance?

  1. A. Roosevelt's political genius lay in synthesizing elements of both left and right criticism into a coherent program, borrowing regulatory ideas from progressives and efficiency principles from business conservatives to create pragmatic policies that maintained political viability by satisfying neither extreme.
  2. B. The New Deal occupied a reform-without-revolution position — it stabilized the capitalist system by addressing its most destabilizing failures without fundamentally redistributing wealth or restructuring ownership — making it an ideologically moderate but historically unprecedented response to economic catastrophe that satisfied neither ideological extreme.
  3. C. The two-sided criticism demonstrates that the New Deal was an incoherent collection of experimental programs lacking a unifying reform philosophy, which is why historians continue to debate whether it extended or shortened the Depression's duration rather than agreeing on a definitive assessment.
  4. D. The New Deal's primary significance was its expansion of the permanent federal bureaucratic apparatus and the precedent it established for government economic intervention, regardless of whether its specific programs effectively accelerated economic recovery from the Depression.
