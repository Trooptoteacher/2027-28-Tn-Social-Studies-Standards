1. What were the "Three Rs" of the New Deal?

  1. A. Regulate, Restrict, and Reorganize
  2. B. Relief, Recovery, and Reform
  3. C. Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle
  4. D. Repair, Rebuild, and Restore

KEY: B · DOK 1 · US.46

Why the key is right: The "Three Rs" of the New Deal were relief, Recovery, and Reform. Roosevelt used those three goals to give immediate help, restart the economy, and change the system after the Great Depression. D is incorrect because it describes a different program, event, or idea than this New Deal policy. It showed how the federal government expanded its role during the Great Depression.

A — Regulation was a real New Deal activity, but it was one instrument of Reform rather than one of the three headings.

C — This is the modern environmental slogan, not a New Deal term. The overlap is the letter R, not the history.

D — These words describe repair generally but name none of the New Deal's three aims: immediate relief, economic recovery, and structural reform.

2. How did the New Deal change the relationship between workers and employers?

  1. A. Fascism promoted extreme nationalism; communism promoted class revolution
  2. B. Desperate populations supported strong leaders who promised stability
  3. C. Gave workers legal rights to unionize, bargain collectively, and strike
  4. D. Refinanced mortgages to prevent foreclosures and keep families in homes

KEY: C · DOK 1 · US.46

Why the key is right: Before 1935 an employer could fire a worker simply for joining a union. The Wagner Act made union membership, collective bargaining and striking legally protected and created the NLRB to enforce them. The change was legal standing, not wages: workers could now negotiate as a body rather than one at a time.

A — This contrasts two European ideologies and has no bearing on the rights American workers gained under the Wagner Act.

B — This describes why authoritarian movements gained support in Depression-era Europe, not what changed for American workers.

D — This is the Home Owners' Loan Corporation's work on mortgages. It helped families keep houses; it changed nothing about bargaining at work.

4. Why did critics of the New Deal charge that FDR's programs represented socialism?

  1. A. Because the New Deal greatly expanded federal regulation of the economy and created public employment and Social Security
  2. B. Because FDR eliminated private property rights by redistributing land from wealthy Americans to the poor
  3. C. Because New Deal programs abolished all private and religious charities in favor of state services
  4. D. Because FDR called for nationalizing all major American industries and placing them under government ownership

KEY: A · DOK 2 · US.46

Why the key is right: New Deal opponents — including business leaders and conservatives — argued that programs like the TVA (a government-owned utility), the AAA (government interference in markets), and Social Security (a government welfare program) crossed the line from capitalism into socialism by making the government a major economic actor.

B — Private property rights were not abolished; New Deal farm policy paid owners to limit production rather than redistributing their land.

C — Private and religious charities continued throughout the 1930s; federal relief supplemented them because their resources had been exhausted.

D — FDR never proposed nationalising major industries. The charge rested on regulation and public employment, not government ownership.

5. Use the photograph to answer the question. This photograph shows a TVA dam, c. 1936. (Photograph, c. 1936. Public Domain.) Evaluate the extent to which the TVA and similar New Deal programs achieved their goals of economic recovery and reform.

  1. A. New Deal programs had no positive effects and actually worsened economic conditions
  2. B. The TVA brought electricity, jobs, and flood control to a poor region, but full economic recovery required the industrial mobilization of World War II
  3. C. New Deal programs completely ended the Great Depression and eliminated poverty in the United States

KEY: B · DOK 3 · US.46

Why the key is right: The TVA successfully electrified the Tennessee Valley, controlled flooding, created thousands of jobs, and improved agricultural practices. However, it also displaced communities and the broader New Deal reduced unemployment from 25% to about 14% — significant relief but not full recovery. The complete end of the Depression came with WWII industrial mobilization.

6. Critics of the New Deal from the left, including Huey Long and socialist intellectuals, argued it preserved capitalism without genuine structural transformation, while critics from the right argued it represented dangerous federal overreach. What does the existence of this two-sided criticism most reveal about the New Deal's actual ideological character and historical significance?

  1. A. Roosevelt's political genius lay in synthesizing elements of both left and right criticism into a coherent program, borrowing regulatory ideas from progressives and efficiency principles from business conservatives to create pragmatic policies that maintained political viability by satisfying neither extreme.
  2. B. The two-sided criticism demonstrates that the New Deal was an incoherent collection of experimental programs lacking a unifying reform philosophy, which is why historians continue to debate whether it extended or shortened the Depression's duration rather than agreeing on a definitive assessment.
  3. C. The New Deal occupied a reform-without-revolution position — it stabilized the capitalist system by addressing its most destabilizing failures without fundamentally redistributing wealth or restructuring ownership — making it an ideologically moderate but historically unprecedented response to economic catastrophe that satisfied neither ideological extreme.
  4. D. The New Deal's primary significance was its expansion of the permanent federal bureaucratic apparatus and the precedent it established for government economic intervention, regardless of whether its specific programs effectively accelerated economic recovery from the Depression.

KEY: C · DOK 3 · US.46

Why the key is right: The fact that Long attacked the New Deal for not going far enough while business conservatives attacked it for going too far locates it precisely in the ideologically moderate reform space: it used federal power to manage capitalism's most destructive tendencies without threatening the fundamental structures of private ownership and profit that left critics demanded be dismantled. 7. Marshall's speech announced what became known as which initiative?

  1. A. The Berlin Airlift to the blockaded city
  2. B. The Eisenhower Doctrine for the Middle East
  3. C. The North Atlantic Treaty Organization
  4. D. The Marshall Plan for European recovery

KEY: D · DOK 1 · US.60

Why the key is right: The Marshall Plan, formally the European Recovery Program announced by Secretary of State George Marshall at Harvard on June 5, 1947, became the initiative the speech launched. Choice B is wrong because the Eisenhower Doctrine came a decade later (1957) and addressed the Middle East, not European recovery. Choice C is wrong because NATO was a military alliance signed in 1949, not the economic-aid program Marshall announced. Marshall Plan aid totaled $13.3 billion to sixteen Western European nations between 1948 and 1952, anchoring containment through economic stabilization.

A — The Berlin Airlift was a 1948-49 supply operation for one city, not a Europe-wide recovery programme.

B — The Eisenhower Doctrine came a decade later, in 1957, and addressed the Middle East.

C — NATO was a 1949 military alliance. The Marshall Plan was economic aid and came first.

10. The Truman Doctrine established what new direction for U.S. foreign policy?

  1. A. Recognition of the Soviet Union as an ally
  2. B. A return to isolationism after World War II
  3. C. A commitment to support nations resisting communist expansion
  4. D. Withdrawal from the United Nations

KEY: C · DOK 2 · US.60

Why the key is right: The Truman Doctrine, articulated in President Truman's March 12, 1947, address to Congress requesting $400 million in aid for Greece and Turkey, established U.S. commitment to support nations resisting communist expansion. The doctrine reframed U.S. foreign policy from regional intervention to a global containment strategy and provided the rationale for subsequent Cold War commitments including the Marshall Plan, NATO, and Korean War involvement. Choice B is wrong because the doctrine explicitly rejected isolationism. Choice A is wrong because Truman cast the Soviet Union as the principal adversary the doctrine was designed to counter. Choice D is wrong because the United States was a founding member and host of the United Nations.

A — The Soviet Union had been a wartime ally, and the doctrine marks the point at which that relationship was publicly abandoned.

B — The doctrine did the reverse: it committed the United States to peacetime intervention abroad and ended the interwar isolationist position.

D — The United States remained in the United Nations and used it during the Cold War, including in Korea.

  2. B. The lack of adequate explosives technology meant engineers had to rely entirely on hand-dug excavations.

KEY: A · DOK 2 · US.23

Why the key is right: One of the greatest obstacles to building the Panama Canal was tropical disease — particularly yellow fever and malaria, which killed thousands of French workers in the earlier failed attempt.
