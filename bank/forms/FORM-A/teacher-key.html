1. What did the Reconstruction Finance Corporation (RFC) do under Hoover?

  1. A. Raised tariffs to the highest levels in U.S. history, worsening the global Depression
  2. B. Loaned government money to banks, railroads, and large businesses to prevent failures
  3. C. Businesses cut wages and jobs anyway to survive; voluntary measures had no enforcement
  4. D. WWI veterans demanding early bonus payment; Hoover ordered military to disperse them

KEY: B · DOK 1 · US.46

Why the key is right: The RFC was Hoover's main federal intervention: it lent public money to banks, railroads and large firms on the theory that keeping big institutions solvent would stop failures cascading down to workers. That 'top-down' logic is why the RFC is the standard contrast with the New Deal's direct relief.

A — Raising tariffs was the Hawley-Smoot Tariff of 1930, a separate Hoover-era policy that restricted trade rather than lending money.

C — This describes the failure of Hoover's voluntary-cooperation approach, in which he asked business to hold wages. The RFC was the opposite: a funded federal lender.

D — This is the Bonus Army episode of 1932, a protest by WWI veterans. It is an event, not an agency, and had no lending function.

2. What was the Bonus Army and how did Hoover respond?

  1. A. Businesses cut wages and jobs anyway to survive; voluntary measures had no enforcement
  2. B. Raised tariffs to the highest levels in U.S. history, worsening the global Depression
  3. C. WWI veterans demanding early bonus payment; Hoover ordered military to disperse them
  4. D. Loaned government money to banks, railroads, and large businesses to prevent failures

KEY: C · DOK 1 · US.46

Why the key is right: The Bonus Army and how did Hoover respond was WWI veterans demanding early bonus payment; Hoover ordered military to disperse them, which made Hoover seem harsh toward struggling veterans. Choice B is wrong because it does not describe the veterans' demand for early bonus payments, and choice C is wrong because it does not describe the veterans' demand for early bonus payments. Public frustration with Hoover helped shift many voters toward the New Deal in 1932.

A — This describes why Hoover's voluntary wage-maintenance appeals failed, not the veterans' bonus demand.

B — This is the Hawley-Smoot Tariff, a trade policy unrelated to the veterans' march.

D — This is the Reconstruction Finance Corporation, a lending agency, not a protest.

3. Use the photograph to answer the question. This photograph shows a TVA dam completed during the New Deal era. (Photograph, c. 1936. Public Domain.) How did New Deal programs like the TVA, shown in this photograph, represent a change in the role of the federal government?

  1. A. The government reduced its involvement in the economy and allowed private industry to manage recovery
  2. B. The federal government's role remained unchanged from the policies of the 1920s
  3. C. The TVA was a private utility corporation that operated without any federal funding or oversight
  4. D. The federal government took direct responsibility for economic development and public welfare

KEY: D · DOK 2 · US.46

Why the key is right: The TVA represented a dramatic expansion of federal power. For the first time, the government directly owned and operated an electric utility, planned regional economic development, and employed thousands of workers. This reflected FDR's philosophy that the government had a responsibility to ensure economic security and development.

A — The TVA is the clearest case of the opposite: the federal government built and ran dams and sold the power itself.

B — Federal public works on this scale had no 1920s precedent; that discontinuity is the point of the image.

C — The TVA was created by Congress in 1933 as a federally owned corporation. Reading it as private misses why critics called it socialism.

4. Why did critics of the New Deal charge that FDR's programs represented socialism?

  1. A. Because the New Deal greatly expanded federal regulation of the economy and created public employment and Social Security
  2. B. Because FDR eliminated private property rights by redistributing land from wealthy Americans to the poor
  3. C. Because New Deal programs abolished all private and religious charities in favor of state services
  4. D. Because FDR called for nationalizing all major American industries and placing them under government ownership

KEY: A · DOK 2 · US.46

Why the key is right: New Deal opponents — including business leaders and conservatives — argued that programs like the TVA (a government-owned utility), the AAA (government interference in markets), and Social Security (a government welfare program) crossed the line from capitalism into socialism by making the government a major economic actor.

B — Private property rights were not abolished; New Deal farm policy paid owners to limit production rather than redistributing their land.

C — Private and religious charities continued throughout the 1930s; federal relief supplemented them because their resources had been exhausted.

D — FDR never proposed nationalising major industries. The charge rested on regulation and public employment, not government ownership.

7. Use the photograph to answer the question. This photograph shows American soldiers during the Korean War, c. 1950. U.S. forces were deployed to South Korea as part of a United Nations coalition after North Korean forces invaded South Korea in June 1950. (U.S. Army photograph, c. 1950. Public Domain.) The deployment of American troops shown in this photograph was part of the U.S. strategy of what?

  1. A. Imperialism
  2. B. Containment
  3. C. Détente
  4. D. Isolationism

KEY: B · DOK 1 · US.60

Why the key is right: The Korean War was a direct application of the U.S. containment policy, which aimed to prevent the spread of communism beyond its existing borders. When communist North Korea invaded South Korea, President Truman committed American forces to stop the expansion, consistent with the Truman Doctrine.

A — Imperialism means acquiring territory. The stated aim in Korea was to hold a line, not to annex it.

C — Détente was the 1970s easing of tensions under Nixon, two decades after Korea and opposite in posture.

D — Isolationism is the refusal to intervene abroad. Sending troops to Korea is its contradiction.

8. Marshall's speech announced what became known as which initiative?

  1. A. The Berlin Airlift to the blockaded city
  2. B. The Eisenhower Doctrine for the Middle East
  3. C. The Marshall Plan for European recovery
  4. D. The North Atlantic Treaty Organization

KEY: C · DOK 1 · US.60

Why the key is right: The Marshall Plan, formally the European Recovery Program announced by Secretary of State George Marshall at Harvard on June 5, 1947, became the initiative the speech launched. Choice A is wrong because the Eisenhower Doctrine came a decade later (1957) and addressed the Middle East, not European recovery. Choice B is wrong because NATO was a military alliance signed in 1949, not the economic-aid program Marshall announced. Marshall Plan aid totaled $13.3 billion to sixteen Western European nations between 1948 and 1952, anchoring containment through economic stabilization.

A — The Berlin Airlift was a 1948-49 supply operation for one city, not a Europe-wide recovery programme.

B — The Eisenhower Doctrine came a decade later, in 1957, and addressed the Middle East.

D — NATO was a 1949 military alliance. The Marshall Plan was economic aid and came first.

9. Read the excerpt below. "The Grand Alliance between the United States, Great Britain, and the Soviet Union rests upon one thing only — our common enemy. When Hitler is defeated, I wonder what will hold us together." — Diplomat George Kennan, Private Memo, 1944 Which of the following best describes the fragility of the World War II Allied alliance referenced in this excerpt?

  1. A. Personal conflicts between Roosevelt, Churchill, and Stalin made sustained military cooperation nearly impossible.
  2. B. The creation of the United Nations divided the Allies into competing blocs before the war even ended.
  3. C. The three Allies had agreed to share all military technology, creating economic competition that strained relations.
  4. D. The Alliance was held together only by a common enemy, masking ideological differences that surfaced once Germany was defeated

KEY: D · DOK 2 · US.60

Why the key is right: Kennan's prescient observation captures the fundamental weakness of the wartime Allied coalition: the U.S., Britain, and the Soviet Union had vastly different political systems, ideological commitments, and postwar goals. The only thing binding them was a common enemy. Once Germany was defeated, these differences rapidly emerged, leading to the Cold War. The ideological conflict between Western liberal democracy and Soviet communism made postwar cooperation impossible. Option A is incorrect because the differences were ideological and political, not merely personal between leaders. Option B is incorrect because economic cooperation was limited and strained even during the war. Option C is incorrect because the UN was created to manage these tensions, not a cause of the breakdown.

A — The source points to ideology, not personalities. The three leaders in fact sustained working cooperation through the war.

B — The United Nations was designed to keep the Allies cooperating after 1945, and the blocs hardened afterward rather than before the war ended.

C — The Allies did not share all military technology — the United States withheld the atomic programme from the Soviets entirely.

16. Read the excerpt below. "The construction of the canal required the labor of tens of thousands of workers — Jamaicans, Barbadians, Spanish workers — in brutal tropical conditions. Yellow fever and malaria killed thousands before Dr. Gorgas brought the diseases under control." — David McCullough, The Path Between the Seas, 1977 Which of the following best describes a major challenge that had to be overcome to complete the Panama Canal?

  1. A. Tropical disease, especially yellow fever and malaria, killed thousands of workers until a mosquito-control campaign cut the death rate
  2. B. The lack of adequate explosives technology meant engineers had to rely entirely on hand-dug excavations.
  3. C. The engineers had to redirect four major rivers that flooded the construction site annually.
  4. D. The project faced a severe labor shortage because American workers refused to work in tropical conditions.

KEY: A · DOK 2 · US.23

Why the key is right: One of the greatest obstacles to building the Panama Canal was tropical disease — particularly yellow fever and malaria, which killed thousands of French workers in the earlier failed attempt. Dr. William Gorgas implemented a massive mosquito eradication campaign based on the theory that mosquitoes spread disease (then a new scientific understanding), which dramatically reduced the death rate and made the construction feasible. Option A is incorrect because the engineering challenge was the mountainous terrain, not river flooding (though landslides were a problem). Option B is incorrect because labor was plentiful — it was disease, not shortage of workers, that was the main challenge. Option C is incorrect because dynamite was readily available — the explosive used was a technological advance, not a limitation.

B — Dynamite and steam shovels were used extensively; the Culebra Cut was blasted, not dug by hand.

C — The Chagres River was dammed rather than redirected, and it was one river, not four.

D — There was no labour shortage: tens of thousands of Caribbean and European workers were recruited, which is what the source describes.
